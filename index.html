Food & drink

Local cuisine

Hungarian cuisine is characterized by its bold, hearty flavors and the generous use of Hungarian paprika, sour cream, and onion. It is a rich, meat-heavy cuisine often considered the spiciest in Europe.

Photo
Gulyás

Paprika beef soup with roots—not the thick “goulash” stew abroad.

Photo
Halászlé

Bright, peppery fisherman's soup with carp or catfish; Szeged-style is famous.

Photo
Pörkölt & nokedli

Slow-cooked meat ragù with hand-torn egg dumplings; kin to paprikás csirke.

Photo
Lángos

Crisp fried dough, garlic rub, then tejföl and grated cheese—market staple.

Photo
Kürtőskalács

Chimney cake from embers: caramelized sugar, cinnamon, or crushed nuts.

Photo
Fröccs & ruin bars

Wine spritzer culture in the Jewish Quarter—courtyards, DJs, and local craft taps.

Food & drink

Local cuisine

Małopolska cooking leans sour and savory: rye starters, forest mushrooms, cabbage, and smoked highland cheese—Rynek stalls and Kazimierz cellars are made for grazing.

Photo
Pierogi

Filled dumplings—ruskie potato-cheese, sauerkraut-mushroom, or sweet bilberry for dessert.

Photo
Żurek

Sour rye soup with white sausage and egg—often ladled into an edible bread bowl.

Photo
Bigos

Hunter’s stew of sauerkraut, fresh cabbage, meats, and prunes—better after a day of reheating.

Photo
Obwarzanek

Chewy braided ring, boiled then baked—sprinkled with poppy, sesame, or salt; Kraków’s street signature.

Photo
Zapiekanka

Halved baguette toasted with mushrooms, cheese, and ketchup—late-night fuel from Plac Nowy kiosks.

Photo
Oscypek

Smoked sheep’s-milk cheese from the Tatras—grilled and served with cranberry jam at markets.

Food & drink

Local cuisine

Bohemian cooking is built on knedlíky (dumplings), slow meats, and caraway-scented sauces—pair plates with tank-drawn pale lager from Czech breweries.

Photo
Svíčková

Marinated beef in creamy root-vegetable sauce, cranberry, and bread dumplings—a Sunday-lunch icon.

Photo
Vepřo knedlo zelo

Roast pork, bread dumplings, and stewed sauerkraut—the Czech comfort trinity on every pub menu.

Photo
Guláš

Paprika-rich beef stew, thicker than Hungarian soup—sopped up with knedlíky or rye bread.

Photo
Smažený sýr

Breaded fried Edam or Hermelín with tartar sauce and chips—beer-hall guilty pleasure.

Photo
Trdelník

Sweet dough wrapped on a spit, grilled, rolled in sugar and nuts—tourist lanes, still a warm treat.

Photo
České pivo

Pale lagers (10°–12°) from tank or tap—Pilsner style born here; ask for “pivo, prosím.”

Food & drink

Local cuisine

Viennese cooking pairs Habsburg roasts and clarified-butter classics with Kaffeehaus ritual—expect jewel-box cakes, whipped cream, and precise service.

Photo
Wiener Schnitzel

Veal pounded thin, breaded, fried in butter—lemon wedge, potato salad or parsley potatoes on the side.

Photo
Tafelspitz

Boiled beef brisket at the simmer—served with minced apple-horseradish, spinach, and roesti.

Photo
Sachertorte

Dense chocolate cake, thin apricot jam, dark glaze—invented here; debate Hotel Sacher vs Demel.

Photo
Apfelstrudel

Paper-thin pastry wrapped around spiced apples, raisins, and breadcrumbs—vanilla sauce optional.

Photo
Kaiserschmarrn

Shredded fluffy pancake with rum-soaked raisins, icing sugar, and plum compote—coffee-house staple.

Photo
Melange

Espresso lengthened with steamed milk, like a cappuccino—order with a glass of water and linger.
